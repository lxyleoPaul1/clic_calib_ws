\subsection{Experimental setup}
The field campaign uses two roadside Ruby-128 LiDARs at 10\,Hz on diagonal
intersection posts ($\sim$70\,m baseline, 3--6\,m mounting height, near-zero FOV
overlap), and a DJI M3E + RTK UAV with no onboard LiDAR.

\subsection{Sphere-anchor truth protocol (evaluation only)}
Static matte spheres provide the ground-truth ruler.
Sphere centers in the world frame are surveyed by total station (millimeter-scale
truth), while sphere centers in each LiDAR frame are obtained from the sealed
point-cloud sphere extraction and center-fitting chain.
An Umeyama alignment between surveyed sphere centers and LiDAR-frame fitted centers
gives an independent reference pose $T_{L_\ell W}^{\mathrm{sphere}}$ for each post.
The board-free pipeline is then run once on UAV body clusters only, producing
$T_{L_\ell W}^{\mathrm{bf}}$.
We report $\Delta$trans, $\Delta$rot, and center-reg between
$T_{L_\ell W}^{\mathrm{bf}}$ and $T_{L_\ell W}^{\mathrm{sphere}}$.
The two pipelines do not share target observations or estimator state: spheres define
truth, not calibration inputs.

\subsection{Coherent-bias replay protocol}
Campaign D tests the relative-extrinsic common-mode claim without requiring repeated
flights.
The raw base-station observations (e.g., D-RTK 2) are retained; the same UAV data are
reprocessed after deliberately offsetting the base-station coordinates by a known
horizontal/vertical vector.
This replay injects a known coherent RTK bias into both sector trajectories.
We compare the induced drift of the two absolute poses with the drift of the composed
relative pose and report the rel/abs trace ratio against the simulation reference
(0.247$\pm$0.12$\times$).
Network RTK logs, when available, serve as a cross-check rather than the primary
coherent-bias experiment.
