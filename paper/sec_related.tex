\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Calibration architectures and roadside deployment}
Continuous-time trajectory representations with batch factor-graph optimization are
standard for multi-sensor extrinsic calibration
\cite{furgale2013unified,rehder2016kalibr}.
We use this lineage for RTK-anchored trajectory fitting, but deliberately separate
shared Stage-1 from independent per-LiDAR Stage-2 solves.
Infrastructure sensing motivates long-baseline, low-overlap roadside layouts
\cite{yu2022dairv2x}; unlike work that assumes overlap or shared targets, we target
the diagonal-post regime ($\sim$70\,m baseline, negligible FOV overlap).

\subsection{Board-free vs.\ target and trajectory matching}
Target-based LiDAR--camera/LiDAR--IMU calibration remains the default
\cite{furgale2013unified,rehder2016kalibr}; here the UAV hull is the calibration
target, while spheres are field evaluation rulers only (Sec.~\ref{sec:field}).
Trajectory-matching methods align sensor frames through shared motion
\cite{ren2023trajmatch}; our difference is the failure mechanism: even with a perfect
trajectory, centroid-only board-free calibration fails without attitude excitation.

\subsection{Relation to prior work on degeneracy}
Anonymous prior work on coplanar multi-LiDAR setups identifies \emph{geometric rank
deficiency} in joint Fisher information when sensors and the body path are nearly
coplanar (simulation $\lambda_{\min}\approx 0$ vs.\ multi-layer $\lambda_{\min}=1.074$;
Sec.~\ref{sec:sim}).
This paper addresses a different near-null direction: aspect-concentrated centroid
bias aligns with translation ($\sim$211\,mm in simulation), and joint lever-arm
optimization cannot remove it (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism_identifiability}).
Thus coplanar geometry and board-free bias--translation collinearity are distinct
failure modes (Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism_coplanar}).
