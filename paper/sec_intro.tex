\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

One would expect the accuracy of a board-free calibration to be governed by LiDAR
range noise and the centimeter-level fixes of RTK.
It is not.
Accuracy is instead set by a quantity rarely treated as a design variable: how
\emph{diversely the UAV attitude is excited} during the calibration flight---\emph{board-free
calibration accuracy is determined by flight attitude diversity, not by the sensor.}

We use \emph{board-free} to mean no dedicated calibration target beyond the UAV
airframe itself---no boards, spheres, or surveyed markers in the calibration loop
(spheres appear only as evaluation rulers, Sec.~\ref{sec:field}).

The body centroid used for board-free alignment is not a fixed offset.
It is an \emph{aspect-dependent bias vector field} on the relative bearing $u_B$.
When aspect is concentrated, this field aligns with extrinsic translation until the
two become \emph{indistinguishable}: a structural degeneracy that silently corrupts
estimates ($\sim$211\,mm in simulation) and that joint lever-arm optimization cannot
remove.
Only active 3D attitude excitation---sector POI tidal-lock combined with pitch and
yaw diversity---decorrelates the field so its world-frame mean can be estimated and
subtracted, recovering centimeter-level accuracy ($\sim$30\,mm mean$\pm$std over
seeds in simulation; best-seed anchor 18.5\,mm).
In the zero-diversity limit, extrinsic translation is strictly unidentifiable
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism_identifiability}); attitude diversity restores
identifiability and accuracy improves monotonically with it
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(c)).
Active 3D attitude excitation is therefore a \textbf{prerequisite} for unbiased
board-free calibration rather than an optional refinement.

Cooperative perception at urban intersections increasingly relies on multiple
\textbf{roadside} LiDARs on diagonal posts with $\sim$70\,m baselines and negligible
field-of-view overlap---settings where calibration boards and shared views are
impractical.
A UAV carrying RTK only (no onboard LiDAR) flies through the combined visibility of
those fixed sensors; each roadside LiDAR observes the UAV hull, enabling
\emph{board-free} calibration from body point clouds alone.
In this regime the attitude-excitation finding is not academic: without deliberate
flight diversity, sub-decimeter extrinsics are structurally unattainable regardless
of sensor grade.

\subsection{Contributions}
We contribute:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{separable two-stage framework}: Stage-1 fits an RTK-anchored UAV
    trajectory $T_{WB}(t)$; Stage-2 freezes the trajectory and calibrates each
    roadside LiDAR pose $T_{L_\ell W}$ in an independent solve---enabling diagonal
    multi-post deployment with zero FOV overlap and no UAV-mounted sensors.
  \item A \textbf{board-free identifiability mechanism} (core contribution): we show
    that aspect-dependent centroid bias and extrinsic translation become collinear in
    the Fisher information under concentrated attitude, yielding a structural
    non-identifiability that no joint estimator can break; active 3D attitude excitation
    restores identifiability, and an aspect gate provides a principled fallback when
    excitation is insufficient (simulation).
  \item A \textbf{relative-extrinsic common-mode dichotomy}: under a coherent systematic
    RTK bias assumption, $\mathrm{Cov}(T_{\mathrm{rel}})$ scales as 0.166$\times$
    absolute covariance, whereas white per-epoch noise composes at 3.101$\times$
    (simulation, scoped).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Sphere targets (scope boundary).}
Retroreflective spheres score board-free error in field experiments only; they are
not a calibration fallback (Sec.~\ref{sec:field}).
