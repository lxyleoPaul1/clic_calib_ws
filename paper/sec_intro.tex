\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

One would expect board-free calibration accuracy to be set by LiDAR range noise and
centimeter-level RTK fixes.
It is not.
It is set by a quantity rarely treated as a design variable: how \emph{diversely the
UAV attitude is excited} during the calibration flight.
In board-free calibration, flight attitude diversity, not sensor grade, determines
whether centimeter-level extrinsics are attainable.

We use \emph{board-free} to mean no dedicated calibration target beyond the UAV
airframe itself---no boards, spheres, or surveyed markers in the calibration loop
(spheres appear only as evaluation rulers, Sec.~\ref{sec:field}).

The body centroid used for alignment is not a fixed offset.
It is an \emph{aspect-dependent bias vector field} on the relative bearing $u_B$.
When aspect and attitude are concentrated, this field becomes collinear with
extrinsic translation: a structural degeneracy that silently corrupts estimates
($\sim$211\,mm in simulation) and that joint lever-arm optimization cannot remove.
Active 3D attitude excitation---sector POI tidal-lock plus pitch and yaw
diversity---decorrelates the field, makes its observed mean estimable, and recovers
centimeter-level accuracy ($\sim$30\,mm mean$\pm$std over seeds in simulation;
best-seed anchor 18.5\,mm).
In the zero-diversity limit, extrinsic translation is strictly unidentifiable
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism_identifiability}); with increasing attitude diversity,
identifiability and accuracy recover smoothly (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(c)).
Active 3D attitude excitation is therefore a \textbf{prerequisite} for unbiased
board-free calibration.

Cooperative perception at urban intersections increasingly relies on multiple
\textbf{roadside} LiDARs on diagonal posts with $\sim$70\,m baselines and negligible
field-of-view overlap---settings where calibration boards and shared views are
impractical.
At these baselines, extrinsic errors directly affect fusion and association.
A UAV carrying RTK only (no onboard LiDAR) flies through the combined visibility of
those fixed sensors; each roadside LiDAR observes the UAV hull, enabling
\emph{board-free} calibration from body point clouds alone.
In this regime the attitude-excitation finding is not academic: without deliberate
flight diversity, sub-decimeter extrinsics are structurally unattainable regardless
of sensor grade.

\subsection{Contributions}
We contribute:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We implement a \textbf{separable two-stage framework}: Stage-1 fits an
    RTK-anchored UAV trajectory $T_{WB}(t)$, and Stage-2 freezes that trajectory to
    calibrate each roadside LiDAR pose $T_{L_\ell W}$ in an independent solve,
    enabling diagonal multi-post deployment with zero FOV overlap and no UAV-mounted
    sensors.
  \item We identify the \textbf{board-free observability mechanism}: aspect-dependent
    centroid bias and extrinsic translation become collinear under concentrated
    attitude, causing structural non-identifiability; active 3D attitude excitation
    restores identifiability, and an aspect gate provides a principled fallback when
    excitation is insufficient (simulation).
  \item We quantify a \textbf{relative-extrinsic common-mode dichotomy}: under a
    coherent systematic RTK bias assumption, $\mathrm{Cov}(T_{\mathrm{rel}})$ scales
    as 0.166$\times$ absolute covariance, whereas white per-epoch noise composes at
    3.101$\times$ (simulation, scoped).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Scope boundary.}
Retroreflective spheres score field error only; they are not a calibration fallback
(Sec.~\ref{sec:field}).
