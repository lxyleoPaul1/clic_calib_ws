\section{Board-Free Mechanism: Aspect-Dependent Bias on $u_B$}
\label{sec:mechanism}

This section explains why board-free calibration succeeds or fails.

\subsection{Bias as a vector field on relative bearing}
At each body-cluster epoch $i$, define the empirical centroid bias
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{b}_{B,i}
  = \hat{\mathbf{c}}_{B,i}^{\mathrm{emp}}
  - \mathbf{L}_{B\to\mathrm{body}}^{\mathrm{nom}},
  \label{eq:bias_field}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{emp}}$ is the centroid of \emph{visible} sampled
points (not the nominal box center plus noise).
The relative bearing $u_B$ lives on the unit sphere; $\mathbf{b}_B$ is a
\textbf{vector field} over flown aspects, not a constant offset.
The observed-mean lever $\mathbf{p}_B$ estimates the \textbf{mean of this field}
over the aspect distribution actually flown:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_B \approx \mathbb{E}_{u_B}\big[\mathbf{b}_B(u_B)\big].
  \label{eq:pB_mean}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Degeneracy as an identifiability statement}
\label{sec:mechanism_identifiability}

Each epoch contributes a world-frame bias driver
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_i = R_{LW}\,R_{WB}(t_i)\,\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{const}},
  \label{eq:frame_bias}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{const}}$ captures the aspect-averaged body-frame bias
structure and $R_{LW}$ maps LiDAR to world.
Consider the mean over the calibration segment:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}
  = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} R_{LW}\,R_{WB}(t_i)\,\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{const}}
  \approx R_{LW}\,\bar{R}_{WB}\,\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{const}}
  \quad\text{when }\{R_{WB}(t_i)\}\text{ is concentrated.}
  \label{eq:mean_v_concentrated}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Concentrated attitude $\Rightarrow$ structural non-identifiability.}
When attitude is nearly constant ($R_{WB}(t_i)\approx\bar{R}_{WB}$), $\bar{\mathbf{v}}$
is a \emph{fixed} world vector.
In the centroid residual, a constant world offset is indistinguishable from a
constant shift of the extrinsic translation $\mathbf{t}_{LW}$: both enter the
observation model as an identical rigid translation of the projected body cloud.
They are therefore collinear in the Fisher information matrix, creating a near-zero
eigenvalue---this is not a noise-floor issue but a \textbf{structural non-identifiability}.
No optimizer, including joint estimation of $\mathbf{p}_B$, can separate bias from
translation from data that excite only this degenerate direction; the concentrated-aspect
joint-opt path is a no-op at $\sim$211\,mm in simulation precisely because it cannot
break this collinearity.

\textbf{Diverse attitude $\Rightarrow$ identifiability restored.}
When $\{R_{WB}(t_i)\}$ spans sufficient pitch and yaw, each epoch rotates
$\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{const}}$ into a different world direction.
Summation averages the horizontal components of $R_{WB}\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{const}}$
($\sim$220\,mm $\to$ $\sim$51\,mm mean horizontal norm in simulation)---diversity in
\emph{attitude}, not translation alone.
A body-$Z$ component $\sim$19.5\,mm remains because yaw cannot average it; observed-mean
$\mathbf{p}_B$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:pB_mean}) explicitly estimates and removes this residual.
Attitude diversity is therefore not merely empirically helpful: it is what restores
the identifiability of extrinsic translation in the presence of an aspect-dependent
centroid bias.
\textbf{Active 3D attitude excitation is a necessary condition for unbiased board-free
calibration.}

\subsection{Engineering consequence: aspect gate and guardrails}
When excitation is insufficient, the system must not pretend $\mathbf{p}_B$ is
identifiable.
An aspect gate (yaw\_cv $\geq 0.65$, with a POI tidal-lock path) attempts
observed-mean refinement only when attitude support is adequate; otherwise it falls
back to centroid-only.
A simulation ground-truth guard further applies observed-mean only if optimization cost
and translation norm do not worsen relative to the centroid solution.
On \flightY{} (low yaw\_cv $=0.32$), the gate closes and the estimate reverts to
centroid-only at 165.0\,mm (simulation)---not a 1243\,mm blow-up.
This is the operational embodiment of the identifiability argument: unidentifiable
$\Rightarrow$ self-aware rollback.

\subsection{Relation to coplanar geometric degeneracy}
\label{sec:mechanism_coplanar}

Prior work on coplanar multi-LiDAR layouts reports a distinct near-zero Fisher
direction: when sensors and the body path are nearly coplanar, joint FIM rank
deficiency yields $\lambda_{\min}\approx 0$ versus $\lambda_{\min}\approx 1.074$
under multi-layer flight (simulation; Sec.~\ref{sec:sim}).
\textbf{That degeneracy is geometric;} the aspect-concentration degeneracy of
Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism_identifiability} arises from \textbf{target bias structure}
(bias collinear with translation under fixed attitude).
The $2\times2$ table (Table~\ref{tab:degeneracy_2x2}) diagnoses the latter;
the $\lambda_{\min}$ audit diagnoses the former.
Both must be avoided in flight design, but they are independent failure modes.
