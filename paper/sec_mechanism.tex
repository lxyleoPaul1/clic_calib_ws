\section{Board-Free Mechanism: Aspect-Dependent Bias on $u_B$}
\label{sec:mechanism}

This section explains why board-free calibration succeeds or fails.

\subsection{Bias as a vector field on relative bearing}
At each body-cluster epoch $i$, define the empirical centroid bias
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{b}_{B,i}
  = \hat{\mathbf{c}}_{B,i}^{\mathrm{emp}}
  - \mathbf{L}_{B\to\mathrm{body}}^{\mathrm{nom}},
  \label{eq:bias_field}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{emp}}$ is the centroid of \emph{visible} sampled
points (not the nominal box center plus noise).
\textbf{Diagnostic use:} Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias_field} is a \emph{diagnostic} definition---
in simulation we evaluate it with ground-truth $T_{L_\ell W}$ to reveal bias
structure; in the estimator, $\mathbf{p}_B$ is an \emph{estimated} parameter and
does not require this GT mapping (avoiding circular dependence).
The relative bearing $u_B$ lives on the unit sphere; $\mathbf{b}_B$ is a
\textbf{vector field} over flown aspects, not a constant offset.
The observed-mean lever $\mathbf{p}_B$ estimates the \textbf{mean of this field}
over the aspect distribution actually flown:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_B \approx \mathbb{E}_{u_B}\big[\mathbf{b}_B(u_B)\big].
  \label{eq:pB_mean}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Degeneracy as an identifiability statement}
\label{sec:mechanism_identifiability}

Identifiability rests on two coupled \textbf{knobs}: (\emph{i})~\textbf{aspect control}
on $u_B$, which determines whether the bias field collapses to a single estimable
vector; and (\emph{ii})~\textbf{attitude diversity} in $R_{WB}(t)$, which determines
whether that vector is separable from LiDAR translation.

\paragraph{Aspect knob (freeze the field).}
When $u_B$ is held nearly constant---POI tidal-lock; $\mathrm{std}(u_B)\lesssim 1.3^\circ$
in our profiles---the body-frame bias field reduces to a constant
$\bar{\mathbf{b}} \equiv \mathbf{b}_B(u_B^*)$.
This is precisely what tidal-lock accomplishes: it freezes the field so that
$\mathbf{p}_B$ has a well-defined scalar-vector target rather than an undefined
mixture over aspects.

\paragraph{Attitude knob (separate bias from translation).}
Each epoch contributes a world-frame bias driver
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_i = R_{LW}\,R_{WB}(t_i)\,\bar{\mathbf{b}},
  \label{eq:frame_bias}
\end{equation}
where $R_{LW}$ is the rotation block of $T_{L_\ell W}$.
Consider the segment mean
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}
  &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} R_{LW}\,R_{WB}(t_i)\,\bar{\mathbf{b}} \\
  &\approx R_{LW}\,\bar{R}_{WB}\,\bar{\mathbf{b}}
  \quad\text{when }\{R_{WB}(t_i)\}\text{ is concentrated.}
\end{split}
  \label{eq:mean_v_concentrated}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Bias--translation collinearity under locked aspect and attitude]
\label{prop:collinear}
Let the body-frame bias be constant ($\bar{\mathbf{b}}$, ensured by aspect control)
and suppose $R_{WB}(t_i)\equiv\bar{R}_{WB}$ for all $i$.
Then for any $\bar{\mathbf{b}}$ there exists
$\delta\mathbf{t}_{LW}=-R_{LW}\bar{R}_{WB}\bar{\mathbf{b}}$ such that replacing
$\mathbf{t}_{LW}$ by $\mathbf{t}_{LW}+\delta\mathbf{t}_{LW}$ leaves all centroid
residuals unchanged; the pair $(\bar{\mathbf{b}},\mathbf{t}_{LW})$ is unidentifiable
along this direction and the Fisher information restricted to it is singular.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Under fixed $R_{WB}$, $\bar{\mathbf{v}}=R_{LW}\bar{R}_{WB}\bar{\mathbf{b}}$ is a
constant world offset in the residual.
Adding $\delta\mathbf{t}_{LW}=-R_{LW}\bar{R}_{WB}\bar{\mathbf{b}}$ applies the
identical rigid shift to the projected cloud; no optimizer can distinguish bias from
translation without breaking either premise.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:collinear} is the \textbf{singular limit} as $R_{WB}$
dispersion tends to zero: when attitude diversity increases, the Fisher information
eigenvalue along the collinear direction grows continuously from singularity and
identifiability is \textbf{smoothly restored}---not via a sharp phase transition.
Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(c) quantifies this recovery with a yaw\_cv sweep
(Sec.~\ref{sec:sim}).

The concentrated-aspect joint-opt floor at $\sim$211\,mm (simulation) is the empirical
signature of Proposition~\ref{prop:collinear}: joint $\mathbf{p}_B$ cannot break the
collinearity when both knobs are ``off.''

\paragraph{Diverse attitude restores identifiability.}
When $\{R_{WB}(t_i)\}$ spans sufficient pitch and yaw, epochs rotate
$\bar{\mathbf{b}}$ into different world directions; summation averages horizontal
components ($\sim$220\,mm $\to$ $\sim$51\,mm in simulation).
A body-$Z$ residual $\sim$19.5\,mm remains (yaw cannot average it); observed-mean
$\mathbf{p}_B$ removes it explicitly.
In the zero-diversity limit the extrinsic translation is strictly unidentifiable
(Prop.~\ref{prop:collinear}); attitude diversity restores identifiability and
accuracy improves monotonically with it (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(c)); active 3D
attitude excitation is a \textbf{prerequisite} for unbiased board-free calibration
rather than an optional refinement.

\paragraph{Mapping to the $2\times2$ table.}
Table~\ref{tab:degeneracy_2x2}: the \textbf{$u_B$ axis} controls whether
$\mathbf{b}_B(u_B)$ collapses to $\bar{\mathbf{b}}$ (POI-lock vs.\ sweep); the
\textbf{$R_{WB}$ axis} controls whether Proposition~\ref{prop:collinear} applies
(concentrated vs.\ diverse attitude).

\subsection{Engineering consequence: aspect gate and guardrails}
When excitation is insufficient, the system must not pretend $\mathbf{p}_B$ is
identifiable.
The \textbf{aspect gate} tests whether attitude support is adequate: it opens if
\textbf{either} yaw dispersion is sufficient (yaw coefficient of variation
$\geq 0.65$) \textbf{or} the trajectory matches a POI tidal-lock pattern (sector
serial lock with near-zero yaw--orbit mismatch).
Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(c) shows a \textbf{smooth} transition in calibration error
vs.\ measured yaw\_cv; the threshold $0.65$ is \textbf{not} a phase-transition
point---it is a \textbf{conservative operating point} on the upper shoulder of that
transition, chosen because below it observed-mean gain is small and seed-to-seed
spread is high, so the solver falls back to centroid-only.
Only when the gate opens is observed-mean refinement attempted.
A simulation ground-truth guard further applies observed-mean only if optimization cost
and translation norm do not worsen relative to the centroid solution.
On \flightY{} (low yaw\_cv $=0.32$), the gate closes and the estimate reverts to
centroid-only at 165.0\,mm (simulation)---not a 1243\,mm blow-up.
This is the operational embodiment of the identifiability argument: unidentifiable
$\Rightarrow$ self-aware rollback.

\subsection{Relation to coplanar geometric degeneracy}
\label{sec:mechanism_coplanar}

Prior work on coplanar multi-LiDAR layouts reports a distinct near-zero Fisher
direction: when sensors and the body path are nearly coplanar, joint FIM rank
deficiency yields $\lambda_{\min}\approx 0$ versus $\lambda_{\min}\approx 1.074$
under multi-layer flight (simulation; Sec.~\ref{sec:sim}).
\textbf{That degeneracy is geometric;} the aspect-concentration degeneracy of
Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism_identifiability} arises from \textbf{target bias structure}
(bias collinear with translation under fixed attitude).
The $2\times2$ table (Table~\ref{tab:degeneracy_2x2}) diagnoses the latter;
the $\lambda_{\min}$ audit diagnoses the former.
Both must be avoided in flight design, but they are independent failure modes.
