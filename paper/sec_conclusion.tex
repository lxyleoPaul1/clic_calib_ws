\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a separable RTK-anchored framework for board-free multi-LiDAR extrinsic
calibration at intersections with negligible FOV overlap.
The central result is an identifiability argument: under concentrated aspect and
attitude, aspect-dependent body-centroid bias is collinear with extrinsic translation,
producing structural non-identifiability ($\sim$211\,mm in simulation) that no joint
optimizer can break.
Active 3D attitude excitation restores identifiability and centimeter-level accuracy
($\sim$30\,mm mean$\pm$std over seeds in simulation; best-seed anchor 18.5\,mm).
An aspect gate operationalizes this insight by rolling back when excitation is
insufficient.
Relative extrinsic uncertainty under coherent RTK bias is substantially attenuated
($\approx$0.25$\times$, simulation, scoped assumption); white noise composes at
$\approx$3.05$\times$.

\textbf{Field validation} on diagonal dual-Ruby data is structured in
Sec.~\ref{sec:field} and remains to be filled.

\subsection{Limitations}
Simulation uses a single M3E box hull; other UAV geometries may alter
$\mathbf{b}_B(u_B)$.
The constant-$\bar{\mathbf{b}}$ premise depends on POI/aspect-control quality, and
poor lock re-expands the field.
The $0.65$ gate is a conservative operating point on a smooth transition
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}(c)), not a phase-transition kink.
Field scoring uses total-station sphere-center surveys with millimeter-scale truth
(Sec.~\ref{sec:field}), but residual uncertainty remains from sphere fitting,
synchronization, survey bookkeeping, RTK drift/colored noise, wind, and POI tracking.
All non-field accuracy claims are simulation-scoped until Sec.~\ref{sec:field} is
filled.

\subsection{Future work}
Field campaigns A--D (Tables~\ref{tab:field_boardfree}--\ref{tab:field_relext});
road-segment and city-scale multi-LiDAR graphs; a unified target framework in which
spheres or boards may become calibration methods for depot-style setups (orthogonal
to this paper's board-free claim); and online FIM / adaptive flight planning with
fresh estimator scopes per mission.
